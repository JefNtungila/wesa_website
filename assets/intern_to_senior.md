# **From Data Science Intern to Hopefully Senior Data Scientist**
```
 
```
## **A broken path**
```
 
```
Someone pointed out that despite my experience, I always seem to be on the lookout for new jobs. That's a valid observation. The reason behind it is that data science is still a relatively new field, which means companies lack a structured framework to help interns grow into senior data scientists. Additionally, they often lack the necessary skills and resources to onboard and mentor junior data scientists. As a result, the junior roles offered in this field often turn out to be dead ends at the same organisation. Other companies do not consider internships towards years of experience.
```
 
```
The transition from a data science intern to a senior data scientist can indeed be a challenging path, especially when compared to other fields like mechanical engineering. Unlike some traditional engineering disciplines where the progression from junior to senior roles is well-defined, the field of data science is relatively new and rapidly evolving. As a result, many companies may not have established frameworks or clear career paths for interns to grow into senior data scientists.
```
 
```
In my experience and research, I have not come across many individuals who have successfully transitioned from data science interns to senior data scientists at the same organisation. Instead, the common paths I have observed involve individuals starting as data analysts or business intelligence engineers and gradually adding machine learning tools and techniques to their skill set. Alternatively, Ph.D. holders may join as senior data scientists directly, skipping the junior level.
```
 
```
Due to the newness of the data science field, organisations lack the necessary frameworks to facilitate the growth of data science interns into senior roles. Additionally, their limited capacity and lack of experience in onboarding junior data scientists is apparent during interviews.
```
 
```
## **Different roles along the way**
```
 
```
### **Role 1: Data Science Intern**
```
 
```
Reflecting on my personal journey, I realised that my path of frequently changing companies was determined when I took on my first data science internship at Capital Enterprise. Despite working with one of my favourite teams, I encountered a recurring issue that is prevalent in this multidisciplinary field: a lack of internal technical data science mentorship. As a summer intern without a clear progression path at the organisation, this amazing experience eventually came to an end.
```
 
```
### **Role 2: Data Science Trainee**
```
 
```
My experience as a data science trainee was a fantastic opportunity that gave me a first-hand understanding of what it means to work as a corporate data scientist. I collaborated with data engineers, DevOps engineers, and BI engineers, gaining valuable technical skills. I had the privilege of being mentored by exceptional data scientists, and I learnt an incredible amount during my time at VF Corporation. Unfortunately, the compensation was quite low, and I couldn't see a straightforward path from the internship to a full-time position as a junior data scientist. In hindsight, this lack of a path existed because the data science field was still new, and the brand-new team was pioneering a role that hadn't been defined before.
```
 
```
### **Role 3: Data Science Research Project**
```
 
```
Working on my postgraduate data science project, I experienced significant growth under the guidance of my academic supervisor. It was during this period that I delivered some of my best work as a data scientist. Naturally, this role, which was part of my master's thesis, would also come to an end.
```
 
```
### **Role 4: Data Science Consultant**
```
 
```
As a data science consultant, stakeholder management became the focal point of my work. Consulting positions throw you into the deep end, with different clients having varied technical stacks such as a combination of Tableau, S3, SageMaker or Power BI, Azure Data Lake Gen 2, and Azure Synapse. Given that clients pay a significant sum for consulting services, there is immense pressure to deliver. Initially, this was quite challenging, and I dealt with stress-related health issues (heartburn, acid reflux). However, I developed lateral skills as a data scientist and gained confidence in working with new technologies.
```
 
```
Aligning with stakeholders, working on data pipelines, conducting data analysis, and delivering insights become the common elements. From a strict data science perspective, all these different technical stacks eventually start to merge. Before being able to deliver value in a data science role, one consistently needs to learn the new technology stack, especially in the consulting industry.
```
 
```
**It's akin to being a musician with perfect pitch, but constantly having to learn a new instrument before being able to play a song.**
```
 
```
Eventually, I expanded my skills laterally to the point where the proposed roles leaned more toward database administration and data engineering. However, my ultimate goal was to grow longitudinally into a senior data scientist position. Sadly, I didn't come across anyone who had progressed from an intern to a senior data scientist at DXC Technology, and I found myself on the bench before being released.
```
 
```
## **Current Situation**

```
Looking ahead, I am excited about delving into Linux scripting, enterprise ML Kubernetes deployments, ML operations, and on-premises dashboard delivery and general Data Science. I would love to deliver turnkey data science solutions, maintain them, and mentor other data science practitioners. I am hopeful that by the end of this journey, I will have established a senior data scientist profile.

## **My favourite interviewing process**
```
 
```
Throughout my journey, I have undergone technical assessments and interviewed with various companies, including REPL, BBC, Vodaphone, EY, The Economist, BNP Paribas Fortis, HSBC, Expedia, Davidson, Dennemeyer, and CausaLens, among others. Among these interviews, my favourite was for a DS II role, which consisted of three rounds. The order and content of the interview process were crucial.
```
 
```
The first round was preliminary with a recruiter, followed by a second round with a technical business manager to discuss my experience and goals. Although the manager had technical knowledge, it was not a technical assessment per se. This approach worked perfectly, as technical terms naturally came up.
```
 
```
Before the third round, I was also asked to complete a take-home assignment. Such assignments are beneficial for data science interviews, as they provide context for the work that would be performed in the role. Whilst I am Hanckerrank certified, I don't recommend solving algorithms as it isn't directly applicable to data science or machine learning positions and lacks contextual relevance. The technical take-home assessment should ideally be part of the later rounds of the interview process and not at the early stages. This allows the candidate to present the assessment to future teammates. Both hard skills and soft skills are then objectively measured during the presentation.
```
 
```
Some companies use the take-home assessment as a way to stall the hiring pipeline whilst they search for a suitable role with their clients for the data scientist position. I advise against this practice.
```
 
```
Although I came in second for the DS II position, their interview process demonstrated that they had a well-defined data science framework in place, with DS I, DS II, and DS III roles. I would recommend setting clear objectives for each stage (DS I, DS II, DS III) to enable internal linear progression every 18 to 24 months.
```
 
```
Furthermore, the lack of career structure in data science means that many data scientists lack a holistic view of the field. This becomes evident when interviewing candidates for different roles such as telecom churn analysis, HR staff attrition analysis, or survival analysis. Technically, these are essentially the same imbalanced classification problem, but hiring managers fail to recognise that.
```
 
```
I hope that the field of data science, along with data engineering, DevOps, and other related fields, matures so that career progression becomes more streamlined. I look forward to updating this post with further developments.
```

## **An ideal Data Science organisational hierarchy framework**
```
 
```
In an ideal data science (DS) framework, the following hierarchy and responsibilities can be envisioned:
```
 
```
- DS I - This level represents interns, graduates, or junior data scientists. They work within a specific scope, primarily focusing on individual contributions (90% IC) while attending meetings (10%). The salary for this level is at a minimum €30K (Western Europe).
- DS II - This level comprises medior or associate data scientists who work independently. They have the authority to set the scope at the project level and handle stakeholder management. The distribution of their work is around 70% individual contributions and 30% meetings. The salary for this level is at a minimum €50K (Western Europe). DS II also mentors DS I.
- DS III - At this level, data scientists are considered senior or consulting managers. They contribute to the strategic aspects of projects and set the scope at the story level. They lead small teams consisting of DS I and DS II members, while also managing stakeholders. The work split is roughly 50% meetings and 50% individual contributions. The salary for this level is around at a minimum €70K (Western Europe).
- DS IV - This level represents DS leads or Principal Data Scientists. Their role predominantly involves stakeholder management and strategy, with only about 10% individual contributions.
- DS V - The highest level in the framework is the Director of Data Science, where the focus is entirely on leadership and management, with 0% individual contributions.
```
 
```
It is important to note that implementing such a comprehensive data science framework, spanning from intern to director, requires a sufficiently large organisation with mature data science processes. Smaller organisations may not have the need or capacity to establish distinct profiles at each level.
