This paper aims to provide a critical analysis of the usage of Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) by law enforcement. The application of computer algorithms to recognise human faces has proven valuable for law enforcement agencies. The analysis is based on a comprehensive review of the existing literature concerning the implementation of FRT by law enforcement entities. The investigation conducted by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) regarding the use of FRT by law enforcement serves as a central theme in this critical examination.
```
 
```
The literature predominantly reflects concerns regarding the legal, social, and ethical implications of employing FRT in law enforcement contexts. Whilst acknowledging these concerns, the paper also explores instances where FRT might offer a more objective alternative compared to human judgement. The analysis delves into the legal, social, and ethical dimensions of FRT's applications in law enforcement, fostering a discussion on the potential societal consequences of its utilisation.
