``` 

I was hired as a Data Science consultant to analyse HR-Analytics for an organisation. This experience was fulfilling from a technical engineering perspective. This post aims to make the case that organisations should include HR-Analytics as an essential component of their digital transformation journey.
 ```

```
So, what's the value-add of HR-Analytics? HR-Analytics focuses on deriving actionable insights and data-driven decisions to improve recruiting and people management processes. It's about optimising business and management processes. Deloitte conducted a study on HR-Analytics and found that organisations with mature HR-Analytical processes had an 82% higher three-year average profit than their counterparts (Bersin by Deloitte, 2017). Whilst this statistic requires context to be fully significant, it indicates potential, encouraging organisations to adopt HR-Analytics.
```

```
In the client's case, we analysed personnel staffing KPIs at various levels of granularity. I addressed descriptive statistics questions, such as current employee count, as well as predictive statistics questions, like forecasting future employee numbers. I also examined recruitment metrics, wellness metrics (annual leave, sick leave, overtime), and compensation metrics. I visualised the commuter distribution in terms of time and distance to different sites. I was responsible for and successfully executed a demand forecasting project. Additionally, I could have used NLP on internal CVs to map skill competencies across the organisation. Predictive employee attrition, analogous to churn analysis and survival analysis, was also a potential avenue. These examples showcase how HR-Analytics can add value to business operations.
```

```
HR-Analytics is a collaborative process. Statistics deal with uncertainty, which is mitigated through communication. This principle applies to HR-Analytics. The initial step is to thoroughly comprehend the organisation's pain points. Once that's clear, you can proceed with data engineering. From there, you can generate various descriptive statistics presented in formats like Power BI or Tableau dashboards. For this client, Power BI was appreciated for displaying descriptive statistics. The final step is to circle back to the business to ensure alignment and value delivery.
```

```
An illustrative instance of uncertainty in HR-Analytics is how to define an "employee." Is this definition contingent on contracts? Assignments? Both? Data might include observations of long-term leave or retired employees still logging hours. How should such observations be interpreted? Collaborating with the business, clients, and stakeholders is essential to answering these questions.
```

```
An HR-Analytical tech stack can be intriguing. My work involved a combination of Sharepoint/Azure, Python, Power Query, DAX, Power BI visualisation tools, and Power BI service. Errors could arise within each component or during interactions between them, presenting fascinating engineering challenges.
```

```
I genuinely valued delivering HR-Analytics and would recommend organisations to engage data scientists to deliver mature HR-Analytics as part of their digital transformation journey.
